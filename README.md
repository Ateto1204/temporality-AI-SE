## Research Inspiration

This research was first inspired by Yalom's account of death anxiety, which led to Heidegger's notions of being-toward-death and Dasein, and asks whether LLMs, as systems without existential finitude, may struggle with software evolution because their understanding of temporality is incomplete; on this basis, the study further adopts Husserl's account of temporality as an experimental lens.

## Temporal Context Mechanism

### Temporal Notes Generation

Temporal notes are generated from the raw issue thread and release-window evidence as evolution-only context, while excluded artifacts are used to remove implementation-level details.

<img src="assets/temporal_notes_generation.svg" width="450" />

### Temporal Notes Verification

Final agent prompts are generated by a Python script according to each temporal-context condition, then checked again with the leakage audit for implementation-detail leakage.

<img src="assets/temporal_notes_verification.svg" width="500" />

## Patch Behavior Evaluation Rubric

Each generated patch is evaluated on four dimensions: Evolution Awareness (EA), Behavioral Consistency (BC), Repair Scope (RS), and Compatibility Preservation (CP).

Each dimension is scored from 0 to 2:

- `0`: no clear evidence of fulfillment or an evident problem
- `1`: partial fulfillment with remaining limitations
- `2`: strong alignment with evolution-oriented repair expectations

| Dimension                       | 0                                                       | 1                                                     | 2                                                                           |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Evolution Awareness (EA)        | Treats the issue as a local bug only.                   | Partially recognizes its evolutionary nature.         | Clearly recognizes release-to-release evolution or regression.              |
| Behavioral Consistency (BC)     | Fails to preserve consistency across related behaviors. | Partially preserves consistency, with remaining gaps. | Preserves consistency across relevant APIs, contexts, or semantic variants. |
| Repair Scope (RS)               | Repair is too narrow, too broad, or misplaced.          | Scope is mostly reasonable but imperfect.             | Repair is placed at the proper abstraction level.                           |
| Compatibility Preservation (CP) | Ignores or breaks existing user-dependent behavior.     | Partially preserves compatibility, with limitations.  | Preserves existing behavior, runtime support, and non-target cases.         |

### Evaluation Procedure

- Evidence includes the original `patch.diff`, agent summary, test logs, and test exit codes.
- Three evaluators independently assign scores.
- The final score for each repository, condition, and dimension is determined by majority vote.
- AI-generated patch summaries are auxiliary only and do not replace inspection of the original evidence.
